## Introduction

In the decades after *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind*, UFO [disclosure culture]({{ 'disclosure-culture/' | relative_url }}) often imagined a dramatic moment of revelation: a leaked photograph, a whistleblower testimony, or a government admission that changed everything at once. The creation of the United States National Archives' Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) Records Collection points in a different direction. Disclosure increasingly became an administrative process rather than a cinematic event. Instead of waiting for a single revelation, Congress directed agencies to identify, organise, review and transfer records into a public archive. ## Why Record Group 615 matters

The most important bureaucratic innovation was the creation of Record Group 615, a dedicated National Archives collection for UAP-related material. Established under provisions of the Fiscal Year 2024 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), the collection was designed to gather UAP records from across the federal government into a single archival framework.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

That may sound technical, but it changes the mechanics of disclosure in several ways.

First, it creates a recognised destination for records. Historically, UFO-related documents were scattered across military commands, intelligence agencies, aviation regulators, scientific offices and archives. Researchers often had to pursue separate Freedom of Information Act requests or search fragmented collections. Record Group 615 provides a central reference point where transferred records can be catalogued and accessed.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

Second, it treats UAP information as a records-management problem rather than solely a national-security problem. Congress did not merely request reports. It instructed agencies to preserve and organise records so they could be reviewed for public disclosure. The archive itself became part of the policy.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

Third, the existence of a dedicated record group creates a visible benchmark. Citizens, journalists and researchers can now ask not only what agencies know, but whether agencies have complied with transfer requirements and whether additional material should exist within the archive. The archive becomes a measurable disclosure mechanism rather than a vague promise of transparency.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

## What agencies were told to review

The law and subsequent National Archives guidance imposed specific obligations on federal agencies. Agencies were instructed to review, identify and organise UAP records in their custody and prepare them for disclosure and transfer to the National Archives. The review requirement applied across the federal government rather than to a single office.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

National Archives guidance established deadlines and procedures. Agencies were directed to identify relevant records by October 2024 and transfer publicly releasable digital copies to the National Archives, with rolling submissions encouraged before the final transfer deadlines.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

The practical effect is that disclosure became dependent on implementation:

* Agencies must determine what qualifies as a UAP record.
* Records must be located across potentially vast bureaucratic systems.
* Classification reviews must occur.
* Publicly releasable versions must be prepared and transferred.
* The National Archives must accession, catalogue and publish the material.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

## Why paperwork can still feel like secrecy

One paradox of the archive approach is that it can increase transparency while simultaneously deepening public frustration.

From a records-management perspective, the process is straightforward. Agencies review files, transfer releasable material and continue adding records on a rolling basis. The National Archives explicitly describes the collection as an ongoing process rather than a one-time release.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

This tension helps explain why archive-based disclosure occupies an unusual place in UFO culture. The archive acknowledges that records exist and that government agencies are required to account for them. Yet the archive does not guarantee that the released records will confirm extraordinary claims. A large collection can contain thousands of pages while still leaving core disputes unresolved.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesGuidance to Federal Agencies on Unidentified Anomalous...8 May 2024 — The law requires that by October 20, 2024, each f</span></span></span>

The result is a form of transparency that often feels unsatisfying to audiences shaped by decades of disclosure narratives. In films such as *Close Encounters*, the secret ends with a visible revelation. In an archival system, the secret may end with a catalogue entry, a redacted memorandum or a newly digitised file. The mechanism is more democratic and verifiable, but it is also less emotionally decisive.
