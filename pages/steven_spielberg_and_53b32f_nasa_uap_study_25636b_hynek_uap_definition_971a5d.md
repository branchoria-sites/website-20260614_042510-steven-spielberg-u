## Introduction

The shift from J. Allen Hynek’s “[close encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }})” to NASA’s “unidentified anomalous phenomena” (UAP) language marks more than a change in terminology. It reflects a change in what question is being asked. Hynek’s system was designed to classify human experiences with UFOs and to make reports comparable. NASA’s UAP framework is designed to determine whether an observation can be identified through scientific evidence and data analysis. That distinction helps explain why Steven Spielberg’s *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* feels very different from NASA’s recent UAP studies: one begins with the witness, the other begins with the quality of the evidence. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportThe study of Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) presents a unique scientific opportunity tha...</span></span></span>

## How Hynek's Categories Shaped Spielberg's UFO Grammar

In 1972, astronomer J. Allen Hynek published *The UFO Experience*, introducing a classification system intended to organise UFO reports into recognisable categories. His framework distinguished distant observations from “close encounters”, which he defined as sightings occurring at relatively short range. The best-known levels became:

* **Close Encounter of the Second Kind**: a sighting accompanied by physical effects, such as marks, interference or environmental traces.
* **Close Encounter of the Third Kind**: observation of occupants or apparent entities associated with the phenomenon. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Allen_Hynek" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">J. Hynek developed the system as a cataloguing tool, hoping to bring greater consistency to UFO reports. Although he remained an astronomer committed to evidence, he believed some cases deserved serious study rather than automatic dismissal. His classification scheme became one of the most recognisable concepts in UFO culture. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_encounter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close encounter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close encounter</span></span></span>

Spielberg adopted that vocabulary directly. Hynek served as a consultant on *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*, and the film took its title from the third category in his scale. Hynek even appeared briefly on screen near the film’s conclusion. ## What NASA Means by Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena

NASA’s modern terminology takes a very different approach. Rather than speaking of encounters, NASA defines UAP as observations of events that cannot presently be identified as aircraft or known natural phenomena. The emphasis is on the status of the observation, not on any presumed explanation. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportThe study of Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) presents a unique scientific opportunity tha...</span></span></span>

The word **“unidentified”** is central. A UAP is not a category of object; it is a category of uncertainty. NASA’s 2023 independent study stressed that the primary challenge is obtaining sufficient, high-quality data to determine what has been observed. The agency concluded that there is no evidence that currently studied UAP represent extraterrestrial technology, while also arguing that unexplained reports deserve systematic scientific examination. It broadens the discussion beyond the classic flying-saucer image and focuses attention on observations that deviate from expectations. NASA’s framework asks questions such as:

* Which sensors recorded the event?
* Can the observation be independently verified?
* Are atmospheric or technical explanations available?
* What additional data would reduce uncertainty? ## Why Changing the Label Changes the Question

The contrast between Hynek and NASA is easiest to see by examining what each framework treats as its basic unit of analysis.
