## Introduction

In *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind* (1977), Steven Spielberg and John Williams accomplished something that remains unusual in mainstream cinema: they transformed film music from commentary into action. The famous five-note motif is often discussed as an iconic theme, but its greater significance lies elsewhere. It operates inside the story world. Scientists play it, aliens answer it, and the success of first contact depends upon it. Rather than serving as emotional accompaniment, music becomes the mechanism through which the narrative advances.

This distinction matters because film scores are normally non-diegetic—they are heard by the audience but not by the characters. *Close Encounters* deliberately collapses that boundary. The same musical material that functions as score also functions as language, signal, protocol and dialogue. By the climax at [Devils Tower]({{ 'devils-tower/' | relative_url }}), the audience is not merely listening to music about communication; it is watching communication occur through music. As the Los Angeles Philharmonic notes in its programme materials on the film, music is presented as “the key to communication between humans and benign aliens,” with the five-note motif serving as the central non-verbal link between the two species.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.laphil.com/musicdb/pieces/1314/excerpts-from-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: laphil.com">[LA Phil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">laphil.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A five-note motif serves as this non-verbal link and appears in various guises</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LA PhilExcerpts from Close Encounters of the Third Kind, John...Music is presented as the key to communication between humans and benign...</span></span></span>

## What Diegetic Music Means in Close Encounters

In film theory, diegetic music is music that exists within the fictional world and can be heard by the characters. A song playing from a radio, a pianist performing in a nightclub, or musicians appearing on screen are all examples of diegetic sound. Traditionally, orchestral film scores occupy the opposite category: they guide audience emotion from outside the story.

Spielberg's innovation was not merely to include diegetic music. Films had done that for decades. The innovation was to make diegetic music the central engine of the plot.

Throughout *Close Encounters*, the audience gradually learns that music is not simply describing the mystery of the UFOs. It is becoming the method through which the mystery will be solved. The five-note phrase appears first as a recurring musical idea, but later emerges as a practical communication system shared by humans and extraterrestrials. The distinction between soundtrack and story therefore dissolves.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thelistenersclub.com/2020/01/03/john-williams-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-a-cinematic-tone-poem/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelistenersclub.com">[The Listeners&#x27; Club]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelistenersclub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">john williams close encounters of the third kind a cinematic tone poem</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Listeners&#x27; ClubJohn Williams&#x27; “Close Encounters of the Third Kind”3 Jan 2020 — Spielberg described the score&#x27;s iconic five-note “Comm...</span></span></span>

Film scholar Tom Schneller's analysis of the score argues that the musical architecture of *Close Encounters* is deeply tied to narrative progression rather than functioning merely as thematic decoration. The score's motifs develop alongside the story's movement toward intelligibility and contact.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274149978_Sweet_Fulfillment_Allusion_and_Teleological_Genesis_in_John_Williams%27s_Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Sweet Fulfillment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sweet Fulfillment - Close Encounters of the Third KindPDF &#124; On Sep 15, 2014, Tom Schneller published Sweet Fulfillment: Allus...</span></span></span>

The result is a rare example of a film where understanding the music is effectively the same thing as understanding the narrative.

## Why Spielberg Needed Music Instead of Language

The film's first-contact scenario creates a practical storytelling problem. Human speech is culturally specific. English, French or any other terrestrial language would be meaningless to an extraterrestrial civilisation.

Spielberg therefore sought a communication medium that could plausibly cross biological and cultural boundaries. Music offered several advantages:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It is pattern-based rather than vocabulary-based.
* It can be represented mathematically.
* It can be translated into light, colour and gesture.
* It allows call-and-response interaction without requiring shared grammar.

</div>

The film's communication system combines tones, coloured lights and hand signals into a structured language-like exchange. The audience sees scientists responding to alien signals through musical phrases rather than spoken words. According to analyses of the film's climax, this system explicitly links pitch relationships with visual and mathematical correspondences, suggesting that both sides are using patterns as a shared foundation for meaning.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thelistenersclub.com/2020/01/03/john-williams-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-a-cinematic-tone-poem/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelistenersclub.com">[The Listeners&#x27; Club]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelistenersclub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">john williams close encounters of the third kind a cinematic tone poem</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Listeners&#x27; ClubJohn Williams&#x27; “Close Encounters of the Third Kind”3 Jan 2020 — Spielberg described the score&#x27;s iconic five-note “Comm...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://musictales.club/article/five-tones-spielbergs-alien-language" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musictales.club">[Music Tales]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musictales.club</span><span class="citation-popover-title">five tones spielbergs alien language</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Five tones of Spielberg&#x27;s alien languageAug 23, 2018 — The most iconic Close Encounters sequence is perhaps the five tones — a simple mus...</span></span></span>

This choice fundamentally alters the dramatic stakes. The question is no longer whether humans can translate alien speech. The question becomes whether two intelligences can recognise and continue a pattern.

## Scientists Become Performers

The climactic sequence at Devils Tower resembles a concert as much as a diplomatic meeting.

Human scientists stand before banks of synthesisers, consoles and signalling equipment. Instead of preparing speeches, they prepare musical responses. Their role is closer to that of performers in an orchestra than negotiators at a conference table.

This transformation is crucial because it places characters directly inside the musical process.

In a conventional science-fiction film, music might swell in the background while scientists make important decisions. In *Close Encounters*, the scientists themselves create the sounds that determine the outcome of events. Every note becomes an action. Every phrase becomes a decision.

The dramatic structure unfolds through successive musical exchanges:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Humans initiate contact.
2. The mothership replies.
3. Humans recognise the reply as meaningful.
4. The exchange becomes increasingly sophisticated.
5. Mutual understanding emerges through repetition and variation.

</div>

The scene therefore generates suspense through musical interaction rather than physical conflict. The audience watches a conversation unfold in real time. The participants happen to be separated by species, but the mechanics resemble improvisational dialogue.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thelistenersclub.com/2020/01/03/john-williams-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-a-cinematic-tone-poem/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelistenersclub.com">[The Listeners&#x27; Club]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelistenersclub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">john williams close encounters of the third kind a cinematic tone poem</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Listeners&#x27; ClubJohn Williams&#x27; “Close Encounters of the Third Kind”3 Jan 2020 — Spielberg described the score&#x27;s iconic five-note “Comm...</span></span></span>

What makes the sequence remarkable is that narrative progression occurs through musical performance itself. If the exchange stopped, the plot would stop.

## The Five Notes Were Designed to Function Like Dialogue

The famous motif succeeds because it is neither a complete song nor a random signal.

John Williams has repeatedly described the challenge of creating a phrase that could plausibly function as communication. Spielberg required a five-note structure, and Williams reportedly generated hundreds of possibilities before arriving at the final version. According to Spielberg's later recollections and Williams's own comments, the phrase works because it feels conversational. Williams explained that it contains both a statement and a question. The melodic shape does not feel finished; it appears to request continuation. This unfinished quality is essential to its narrative function.

A traditional heroic theme announces identity. The *Star Wars* main title tells audiences who the heroes are. The *Jaws* motif announces danger. The *Close Encounters* motif does something different: it invites participation.

The phrase only achieves its purpose when another intelligence answers it.

Because of this design, every repetition advances the story. The motif is not attached to communication as a symbol; it performs communication as an action.

## The Mothership Becomes a Musical Character

Another reason the score becomes plot is that the alien craft effectively acquires a musical voice.

Rather than speaking through translated dialogue, the mothership expresses itself through musical replies. Williams and Spielberg experimented with different sonic approaches during production, initially trying more futuristic synthesised responses before deciding that a richer orchestral voice worked better dramatically. Williams later recalled that both he and Spielberg disliked the earliest electronic responses and revised the approach.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2023/01/steven-spielberg-john-williams-50-years-movie-collaboration" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanityfair.com">[Vanity Fair]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanityfair.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vanity Fair John Williams Is Not Retiring on Steven Spielberg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This event, hosted by the American Cinematheque, took place in front of a packed theater. The consequence is that the mothership behaves almost like a performer in a duet.

Its responses display variation, timing and personality. The craft does not merely echo human signals. It elaborates on them. The audience interprets those elaborations much as it would interpret a human speaker's tone of voice.

This is a significant shift from standard science-fiction representations of alien intelligence. Many films portray communication through machines that translate language after contact has already been established. *Close Encounters* instead makes the communicative process itself the centrepiece of the drama.

The aliens become understandable not because they reveal information but because they participate in musical exchange.

## How the Film Gradually Trains the Audience

One reason the climax works so effectively is that the audience has been prepared for it throughout the film.

The five-note motif does not appear suddenly at Devils Tower. Williams embeds related musical ideas throughout the score. By the time the communication sequence arrives, viewers already recognise the phrase subconsciously.

This strategy creates a remarkable reversal.

At first, audiences assume they are hearing a conventional film theme. Later, they discover that the theme has been functioning as a signal all along. Music that seemed to belong exclusively to the soundtrack turns out to belong to the story world as well.

Critics and scholars frequently point to this dual function as one of the score's most distinctive achievements. ## Why the Soundtrack Literally Becomes the Story

Many films feature memorable music. Far fewer films make music indispensable to narrative causation.

In *Close Encounters*, remove the musical communication sequence and the plot's central problem becomes unsolved. The encounter cannot occur as presented. The five-note motif is therefore not ornamental. It is functional.

The score ceases to be background material and becomes a narrative technology.

As commentators on the film have observed, few major studio productions have ever assigned such structural importance to a musical idea. The five-note motif is not merely the film's most recognisable sound; it is the mechanism that allows humans and aliens to recognise one another as intelligences capable of dialogue.     return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
