## Close Encounters and Fearful Awe

*Close Encounters of the Third Kind* is often remembered for its radiant ending, where humans and aliens communicate through music and light. Yet much of the film is built from fear.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/steven_spielberg_and_53b32f_friendly_vs_hostile_b51dae_friendly_aliens_fear_aead47-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Friendly Aliens Fear Aead 47 illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/steven_spielberg_and_53b32f_friendly_vs_hostile_b51dae_friendly_aliens_fear_aead47-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/steven_spielberg_and_53b32f_friendly_vs_hostile_b51dae_friendly_aliens_fear_aead47-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The first encounters are disturbing rather than comforting. [Roy Neary]({{ 'roy-neary/' | relative_url }})'s roadside UFO sighting overwhelms him physically and psychologically. Elsewhere, young Barry Guiler is drawn towards mysterious lights before being taken from his home during one of the film's most frightening sequences. Spielberg presents the aliens as potentially benevolent, but the people experiencing them have no way of knowing that in advance. The unknown remains genuinely alarming. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

Fear also emerges through obsession. After his encounter, Roy becomes consumed by visions of [Devils Tower]({{ 'devils-tower/' | relative_url }}). His behaviour grows increasingly erratic, and his family cannot understand what is happening to him. Spielberg later reflected that the experience overturns Roy's life and drives him towards complete upheaval. The alien contact is not an inspiring revelation alone; it destroys the stability of his home. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-TerrestrialIt tells the story of Elliott, a boy who befriends an extraterrestrial that he names E.T. who has been stran...</span></span></span>

### Wonder Built on Uncertainty

The film's famous sense of awe depends on uncertainty rather than reassurance.

Viewers spend most of the story without clear answers about the UFOs' intentions. Government [secrecy]({{ 'secrecy/' | relative_url }}), unexplained phenomena and strange compulsions create an atmosphere closer to a mystery thriller than a comforting fantasy. Even when communication finally succeeds, the audience remembers the risks that preceded it: disappearances, panic and social isolation. ## E.T. and the Fear of Capture

*E.T.* is usually described as Spielberg's warmest alien story, but fear is woven through nearly every stage of the narrative.

Spielberg initially introduces E.T. almost like a creature from a horror film. Elliott hears noises in the garden shed, glimpses movement in the darkness and confronts a figure he cannot identify. The audience experiences the alien first as a potential threat before discovering his vulnerability. Spielberg deliberately allows fear and wonder to exist together. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://medium.com/from-director-steven-spielberg/fear-is-the-driving-force-spielberg-and-horror-a0a171421b77" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fear is the Driving Force: Spielberg and Horror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">alien&#x27;s rustling in the shed bodes ill for our human hero. ### Illness, Death and Emotional Vulnerability

The middle section of *E.T.* becomes unexpectedly bleak.

Because Elliott and E.T. share an emotional connection, the alien's physical decline affects the child directly. As E.T. weakens, the film introduces imagery of medical crisis, quarantine and death. Sterile equipment, containment procedures and the apparent loss of E.T.'s life transform a family adventure into a story of grief and helplessness. The emotional impact comes from the fact that the audience has already accepted E.T. as friendly. ## Wonder That Disrupts Family Life

One of Spielberg's most consistent ideas is that alien contact disrupts ordinary relationships, even when the visitors are not hostile.

In *Close Encounters*, Roy's obsession gradually separates him from his wife and children. The aliens never directly attack the family, yet the encounter contributes to its collapse. Critics and commentators frequently point to the domestic strain as one of the film's most emotionally painful elements. The fear is social and personal rather than physical. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.brightwalldarkroom.com/2016/07/11/monsieur-neary-what-do-you-want/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brightwalldarkroom.com">[Bright Wall]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brightwalldarkroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bright Wall/Dark RoomMonsieur Neary, What Do You Want?11 Jul 2016 — There are many spectacular scenes in Close Encounters, but it&#x27;s the s...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.brightwalldarkroom.com/2016/07/11/monsieur-neary-what-do-you-want/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brightwalldarkroom.com">[Dark Room]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brightwalldarkroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bright Wall/Dark RoomMonsieur Neary, What Do You Want?11 Jul 2016 — There are many spectacular scenes in Close Encounters, but it&#x27;s the s...</span></span></span>

In *E.T.*, family disruption takes a different form. Elliott's household is already fragile because of parental absence. E.T.'s arrival brings comfort and companionship, but it also draws the family into danger and secrecy. The children must navigate threats from adults while coping with the possibility of losing someone they love. The alien becomes a catalyst that exposes existing vulnerabilities within the family structure. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://musingsofamiddleagedgeek.blog/2022/06/30/revisiting-steven-spielbergs-[e-t" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: musingsofamiddleagedgeek.blog">[Musings of a Middle-Aged Geek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">musingsofamiddleagedgeek.blog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Musings of a Middle-Aged GeekRevisiting Steven Spielberg&#x27;s “E.T.” after 40 years…June 30, 2022 — 30 Jun 2022 — Elliott reaches out to his...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 30, 2022</span></span></span>

## Why Spielberg Keeps Fear Inside Friendly Contact

The key mechanism in Spielberg's UFO cinema is that friendliness does not eliminate uncertainty. His aliens may arrive in peace, but humans cannot immediately recognise that fact. People react with fear because they are confronted by forces beyond their understanding, control or experience.

This approach distinguishes Spielberg from simpler stories in which aliens are either obvious monsters or obvious saviours. In his most influential UFO films, contact remains emotionally risky. Characters face abduction, obsession, illness, separation and social breakdown before they reach understanding. Fear is not the opposite of wonder; it is part of the route towards it.
