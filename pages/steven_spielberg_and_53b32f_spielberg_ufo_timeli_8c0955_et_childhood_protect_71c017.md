## From witness spectacle to suburban secrecy

*Close Encounters* ended with governments, researchers and enormous spacecraft. *E.T.* deliberately shrank that scale. The story rarely leaves a California neighbourhood, and most of the action unfolds through the perspective of children trying to hide a vulnerable extraterrestrial from adults. The Library of Congress essay on the film notes how unusually confined and domestic its setting is compared with Spielberg’s earlier UFO epic.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-film-preservation-board/documents/ET.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Library of Congressfilm essay for &quot;E.T</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Extra-Terrestrial&quot;May 28, 2015 — Even. This was more than a change of scenery. It transformed the emotional structure of alien contact. In many science-fiction stories, humans fear what the alien might do to them. In *E.T.*, the central fear is what humans might do to the alien. Elliott’s first instinct becomes concealment and protection. The extraterrestrial is not a threat to childhood; childhood becomes the safest place for the extraterrestrial.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.commonsensemedia.org/movie-reviews/et-the-extra-terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commonsensemedia.org">[Common Sense Media]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commonsensemedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Common Sense MediaE.T.: The Extra-Terrestrial Movie Review9 Oct 2025 — While hiding E.T., Elliott develops a close friendship with the al...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span> Spielberg reinforced this perspective through filmmaking choices. Much of the early film is shot from a child’s eye level, and adults are often partially obscured or framed as distant authority figures. The narrative privileges the children’s emotional understanding over institutional control, encouraging audiences to identify with guardianship rather than investigation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span>

## Elliott's loneliness and emotional contact

The friendship between Elliott and E.T. works because both characters are isolated. Elliott is coping with the absence of his father after his parents’ separation, while E.T. has been left behind by his own people. Their bond is therefore built on shared loss before it is built on science-fiction wonder.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nme.com/news/film/steven-spielberg-explains-how-parents-divorce-inspired-e-t-3211364" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nme.com">[NME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nme.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">steven spielberg explains how parents divorce inspired e t 3211364</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Steven Spielberg explains how his parents&#x27; divorce...24 Apr 2022 — Steven Spielberg explains how his parents&#x27; divorce inspired &#x27;E.T.&#x27;. &quot;...</span></span></span>

Spielberg has repeatedly connected the film to his own childhood experience. After his parents divorced, he imagined an alien companion who could fill emotional gaps in his life. Later accounts of the film’s development describe E.T. as emerging from memories of that imaginary friend and from Spielberg’s desire to tell a story about childhood loneliness.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nme.com/news/film/steven-spielberg-explains-how-parents-divorce-inspired-e-t-3211364" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nme.com">[NME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nme.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">steven spielberg explains how parents divorce inspired e t 3211364</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Steven Spielberg explains how his parents&#x27; divorce...24 Apr 2022 — Steven Spielberg explains how his parents&#x27; divorce inspired &#x27;E.T.&#x27;. &quot;...</span></span></span>

The film turns this personal history into a model of contact based on empathy. Elliott does not communicate with E.T. through technology, diplomacy or scientific procedure. Instead, they develop an emotional and even telepathic connection. Critics and commentators have long noted that this bond helps Elliott express feelings he normally suppresses. Contact becomes a form of emotional healing rather than a transfer of information.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span>

Melissa Mathison’s screenplay strengthened this emphasis. Accounts of the film’s creation describe E.T.’s powers and his emotional sensitivity as central to the relationship between the boy and the alien. The extraterrestrial functions less as a visitor from another world than as a companion capable of understanding childhood pain.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.newyorker.com/culture/culture-desk/the-screenwriter-of-e-t-and-the-bfg-says-goodbye" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newyorker.com">[The New Yorker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newyorker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The New Yorker The Screenwriter of &quot;E.T.&quot; and &quot;The BFG&quot; Says Goodbye</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Extra-Terrestrial.&quot; Mathison&#x27;s approach was highly involved, influencing not just the script but the filming method as well, includin...</span></span></span>

## Why vulnerability changed Spielberg's UFO mood

The most radical element of *E.T.* is that the alien is weak.

Science-fiction cinema had often portrayed extraterrestrials as invaders, conquerors or mysterious superior beings. Spielberg instead built a story around an alien botanist stranded far from home, physically fragile and dependent on children for survival. The drama arises from vulnerability rather than power.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span>

This reversal altered the emotional tone of Spielberg’s UFO imagination in several ways:

* **Fear gave way to responsibility.** The audience worries about E.T.’s safety more than humanity’s safety.
* **Wonder became intimate.** Extraordinary events occur in bedrooms, back gardens and school corridors rather than at monumental contact sites.
* **The alien became individual.** Unlike the largely collective extraterrestrial presence in *Close Encounters*, E.T. has a recognisable personality, habits and emotional needs.
* **Contact became reciprocal.** Elliott protects E.T., but E.T. also helps Elliott recover a sense of connection and belonging.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span> The famous flying-bicycle sequence illustrates this change perfectly. In another UFO film, flight might signal superior alien technology or military power. In *E.T.*, it becomes a moment of liberation shared by children.
