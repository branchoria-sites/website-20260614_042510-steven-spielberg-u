## Introduction

In *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind*, Roy Neary’s visions matter because they transform an unexplained UFO encounter into something that can be located on a map. Before he knows the name “[Devils Tower]({{ 'devils-tower/' | relative_url }})”, Roy is haunted by a distinctive mountain shape that repeatedly appears in his thoughts and actions. What begins as a private obsession gradually becomes evidence. When televised footage finally reveals the real Wyoming landmark, the image in Roy’s mind acquires a geographical destination. Spielberg turns a psychological mystery into a navigational one: the vision is not symbolic, but directional. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.intermountainhistories.org/items/show/226" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intermountainhistories.org">[Intermountain Histories]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intermountainhistories.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intermountain HistoriesClose Encounters of the Third Kind and Devils Tower...It begins with Neary forming small structures without conc...</span></span></span>

## Mashed Potatoes, Clay and Repetition

After his close encounter with the UFO, Roy begins seeing the same shape everywhere. The image does not arrive as a verbal instruction. Instead, it manifests through repetition. He absent-mindedly moulds objects into the same form and becomes increasingly unable to ignore it. One of the film’s most famous scenes shows him sculpting a miniature mountain from mashed potatoes at the family dinner table, an act that appears irrational to everyone around him but feels urgently meaningful to him. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.intermountainhistories.org/items/show/226" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intermountainhistories.org">[Intermountain Histories]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intermountainhistories.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intermountain HistoriesClose Encounters of the Third Kind and Devils Tower...It begins with Neary forming small structures without conc...</span></span></span>

The importance of these scenes is not that Roy recognises Devils Tower immediately. He does not. The audience watches him struggle with an image whose meaning remains hidden. Spielberg repeatedly presents the mountain as a visual puzzle:

* He shapes food into it.

The repetition functions as evidence within the story. Roy is not pursuing Devils Tower because he has chosen a destination. He is pursuing a form that has embedded itself in his consciousness. Each recreation reinforces the impression that the image originates outside his ordinary experience.

The growing scale of the models also mirrors the growth of his conviction. What begins as a small gesture at the dinner table develops into an overwhelming attempt to reconstruct an entire landscape inside his home. The mountain becomes the physical record of an obsession that he cannot explain but cannot abandon. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.intermountainhistories.org/items/show/226" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intermountainhistories.org">[Intermountain Histories]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intermountainhistories.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intermountain HistoriesClose Encounters of the Third Kind and Devils Tower...It begins with Neary forming small structures without conc...</span></span></span>

## The Television Recognition Scene

The breakthrough occurs not in the sky but through television.

After weeks of compulsive modelling, Roy sees a news report connected to the government's evacuation operation near Devils Tower. The broadcast includes images of the Wyoming landmark. For the first time, the mysterious shape in his mind is matched to a real location. Roy immediately recognises it as the mountain he has been recreating. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/JoshHorowitzMTV/posts/this-is-important-this-means-somethingroy-neary-close-encounters-of-the-third-ki/1565700058731285/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roy Neary, Close Encounters of the Third KindAfter Roy&#x27;s wife leaves him due to his erratic behavior, he sees a TV report about a...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fran-delpizzo.medium.com/the-artist-and-the-obsession-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-e55b963ba1a4?source=---------9----------------------------" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fran-delpizzo.medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fran-delpizzo.medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Steven Spielberg&#x27;s Close Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; MediumSoon after, Roy and Gillian both see a news report on the TV, and...</span></span></span>

## Private Compulsion Becomes Geography

The significance of the television revelation is that it turns an inner experience into a route.

Before the recognition scene, Roy's mountain is an abstract shape. Afterwards, it becomes a destination that can be located, travelled to and reached. The image acquires coordinates.
