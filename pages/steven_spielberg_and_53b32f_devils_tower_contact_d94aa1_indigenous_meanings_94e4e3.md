## Introduction

For many people, [Devils Tower]({{ 'devils-tower/' | relative_url }}) is inseparable from Steven Spielberg’s *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind*. The film turned the Wyoming monolith into one of the most recognisable UFO locations in popular culture. Yet the alien-contact image sits on top of far older meanings. Long before the Tower became a destination for fictional extraterrestrials, it was a sacred place woven into the histories, ceremonies and cosmologies of numerous Indigenous nations of the Plains and Black Hills region. More than two dozen tribes maintain cultural connections to the site today, and many know it not as Devils Tower but through names that translate as Bear Lodge, Bear’s Tipi or related forms.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span>

## Sacred Site Before Film Icon

The landmark that became famous through cinema was already culturally significant long before Hollywood discovered it. The National Park Service notes that many tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills have longstanding relationships with the site, and that it remains sacred to numerous Indigenous nations. Ceremonies, offerings, prayer ties and acts of reverence continue there today.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span>

One reason the UFO image can be misleading is that it encourages visitors to view the Tower as a mysterious object awaiting explanation. Indigenous traditions often approach it differently: not as a puzzle but as a place already embedded within a moral and spiritual landscape. The Tower is significant because of what happened there, what it teaches, and how it connects people to sacred relationships.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span>

This difference matters. In *Close Encounters*, the Tower functions as the endpoint of a summons from elsewhere. In Indigenous traditions, meaning flows from the place itself and from generations of stories, ceremonies and responsibilities attached to it. The site is not important because something arrives from the sky; it is important because it already occupies a sacred position within the world.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span>

## Bear Lodge and Sky Narratives

Many Indigenous stories associated with the Tower centre on a giant bear. Although details vary between nations, a recurring theme involves people—often children or young girls—being pursued by a powerful bear and seeking help through prayer. The rock rises upward, carrying them beyond the bear’s reach. The vertical grooves visible on the Tower’s sides are explained as marks left by the bear’s claws as it attempted to climb after them.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://wyshs.org/devils-tower-bear-lodge" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wyshs.org">[Wyoming]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wyshs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">devils tower bear lodge</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wyoming Historical SocietyDevils Tower (Bear Lodge)Long before it was designated as America&#x27;s first National Monument in 1906, the tower...</span></span></span>

The important point is not whether these stories function as geology in a modern scientific sense. Their significance lies in the relationships they express. The Tower becomes a place where prayer is answered, where the earth responds to danger, and where survival depends upon sacred intervention. The rock is not merely a dramatic backdrop; it acts within the story.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span>

Many versions also contain a strong connection between earth and sky. In some traditions, those rescued by the rising rock eventually become stars, often associated with the Pleiades. The Tower therefore serves as a bridge between terrestrial experience and the heavens. The movement upward is not about escape into outer space but about transformation, protection and cosmic relationship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devils_Tower" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Devils Tower</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Devils Tower</span></span></span>

Other narratives emphasise spiritual ascent in different ways. National Park Service materials describe Lakota traditions involving vision quests and miraculous movement between the base and summit. These stories portray the Tower as a place where ordinary boundaries can be crossed through spiritual power rather than technological intervention.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nps.gov/deto/learn/historyculture/americanindians.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Park Service American Indians</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceAmerican Indians - Devils TowerMany tribes of the Great Plains and Black Hills regions had connections with the area...</span></span></span>

Seen through this lens, the resemblance between Indigenous sky narratives and Spielberg’s fascination with contact is intriguing but limited. Both involve movement between worlds and encounters with something beyond everyday experience. Yet the underlying meanings are very different. ## Seeing the Landmark Through Both Histories

The most revealing way to understand Devils Tower within the broader story of Spielberg and UFOs is to recognise that the film added a new mythology to a place already dense with older ones. *Close Encounters* transformed the Tower into a global symbol of contact, but Indigenous traditions had long treated it as a place where the human world touched larger powers.

The difference is that the older stories are not waiting beneath the UFO image as forgotten curiosities. They remain active traditions connected to living communities. Bears, ascent, stars, prayer and sacred responsibility continue to shape how many Indigenous peoples understand the Tower today. The film made the landmark famous, but it did not create its meaning.       button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
