## Introduction

Within Spielberg’s broader body of UFO and alien stories, this marks a striking shift. Where earlier films often centred on discovery or transcendence, Ray Ferrier’s encounter with the unknown becomes an exercise in responsibility, sacrifice, and endurance. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Worlds_%282005_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">War of the Worlds (2005 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">War of the Worlds (2005 film</span></span></span>

## Why Ray Is Not a Scientist, Soldier, or Chosen Witness

One of Spielberg’s most important decisions was to make Ray an ordinary working-class dockworker rather than a scientist, military officer, government official, or expert investigator. The screenplay intentionally limits the audience to the viewpoint of someone on the edge of events rather than at the centre of decision-making. As a result, Ray never understands the invasion better than anyone else. He simply experiences it. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

This choice transforms the meaning of alien contact. In many science-fiction stories, the protagonist gains knowledge, authority, or a special connection to the extraterrestrial presence. Ray gains none of these things. He receives no revelation and discovers no solution. His value lies in improvisation and persistence. When technology fails, governments collapse, and military resistance appears ineffective, Ray’s usefulness comes from practical decisions: finding transport, identifying danger, securing shelter, and keeping his children moving. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Worlds_%282005_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">War of the Worlds (2005 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">War of the Worlds (2005 film</span></span></span>

The film repeatedly emphasises his shortcomings. He is divorced, estranged from his son Robbie, and clearly uncomfortable with parental responsibility. Critics and commentators have often described him as one of Spielberg’s more flawed protagonists, a man whose deficiencies are visible from the opening scenes. Yet those deficiencies are precisely what make his transformation meaningful. He is not a natural hero discovering courage; he is an inadequate father forced into competence by circumstances. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://blueprintreview.co.uk/2021/10/top-10-spielberg-antiheroes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueprintreview.co.uk">[Blueprint: Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueprintreview.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blueprint: Review Top 10 Spielberg Antiheroes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here we are again with Tom Cruise... ## How Survival Replaces Wonder as His Defining Skill

Earlier Spielberg alien films often derive emotional power from awe. *War of the Worlds* replaces that emotion with fear. Ray’s defining ability is not curiosity but survival.

Throughout the film, he develops a set of skills that have little to do with understanding the invaders and everything to do with protecting Rachel and Robbie:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Recognising when a location is no longer safe.
* Reading crowd behaviour and avoiding panic-driven violence.
* Maintaining movement rather than becoming trapped.
* Shielding his daughter from psychological trauma when possible.
* Making morally difficult decisions under extreme pressure.

</div>

The emphasis on Rachel is particularly important. Dakota Fanning’s performance presents a child overwhelmed by terror, and Ray increasingly becomes responsible not only for her physical safety but also for managing her fear. Some of his most memorable actions involve emotional protection rather than combat. He constantly reassures her, directs her attention away from horrific sights, and attempts to create a sense of security despite having no real control over events. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0407304/characters/nm0000129/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imdb.com">[IMDb]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imdb.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Don&#x27;t look down. Don&#x27;t look around me. I&#x27;m taking you to the car and you&#x27;re gonna want...Read more...</span></span></span>

This is a fundamentally different model of heroism from the scientist who deciphers an alien message or the soldier who defeats an invasion force. Ray rarely wins. He escapes. His victories are measured in hours survived and dangers avoided rather than enemies defeated.

The basement sequence with Harlan Ogilvy illustrates this shift especially clearly. Ogilvy dreams of resistance and revenge, imagining ways to fight back against the invaders. Ray recognises that such fantasies threaten Rachel’s survival. The film’s most controversial moral decision emerges from this conflict, with Ray choosing his daughter’s safety over another adult’s obsession with retaliation. In survival terms, practicality defeats heroics. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

## A Father Rebuilt Through Crisis

The invasion does not reveal Ray’s strengths; it creates them.

Academic analyses of the film have noted that Ray’s paternal rehabilitation depends on catastrophe. Before the attack, he avoids responsibility and struggles to connect with his children. The alien invasion becomes the mechanism through which he gradually earns their trust. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/105219/1/War%20of%20the%20Worlds%20HH.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: orca.cardiff.ac.uk">[ORCA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">orca.cardiff.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">War of the Worlds HH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This is not to say that thematizing troubled fatherhood is specific to Spielberg.Read more...</span></span></span>

Several moments demonstrate this progression:

**At the beginning**, Robbie openly resents him and Rachel relies more on her brother than on her father.

**During the journey**, Ray increasingly becomes the person both children depend upon for decisions and protection.

**By the end**, his identity is no longer defined by his failures as an ex-husband but by his actions as a father under impossible conditions.

Importantly, Spielberg does not portray this growth as graceful or inspirational. Ray remains frightened, confused, and frequently overwhelmed. His development feels credible because it occurs through repeated failures, mistakes, and desperate adaptations rather than sudden moral enlightenment. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/105219/1/War%20of%20the%20Worlds%20HH.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: orca.cardiff.ac.uk">[ORCA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">orca.cardiff.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">War of the Worlds HH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This is not to say that thematizing troubled fatherhood is specific to Spielberg.Read more...</span></span></span>

The result is one of Spielberg’s most grounded portraits of fatherhood. Ray is not transformed into an ideal parent. He becomes a functional one.

## The Darker Mirror of Roy Neary Leaving Home

The most revealing comparison within Spielberg’s UFO cinema is not with soldiers or scientists but with [Roy Neary]({{ 'roy-neary/' | relative_url }}) from *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind*. Both men are ordinary working-class fathers whose lives are disrupted by contact with the unknown. Yet their responses move in opposite directions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

Roy Neary is drawn away from domestic life. His encounter with the UFO phenomenon becomes an obsession that ultimately separates him from his family. Spielberg presents his journey as a pursuit of transcendence, mystery, and personal destiny. Roy follows the aliens because they represent something larger than ordinary existence. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2006/great-directors/spielberg/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sensesofcinema.com">[Senses of Cinema]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sensesofcinema.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Senses of CinemaSpielberg, Steven7 Feb 2006 — When Close Encounters becomes a special-effects extravaganza in its final act, Spielberg is...</span></span></span>

Ray Ferrier experiences the inverse trajectory.

Instead of leaving his family to pursue the unknown, he clings to his family while fleeing it. The alien presence narrows his world rather than expanding it. Every decision becomes focused on keeping Rachel and Robbie alive. The cosmos offers no calling and no higher purpose. It offers only danger.

This contrast reflects a broader change in Spielberg’s treatment of extraterrestrial contact. Roy’s story asks what happens when a man abandons home in pursuit of wonder. Ray’s story asks what happens when a man discovers that home is the only thing worth protecting.

Seen this way, *War of the Worlds* functions as a dark reflection of *Close Encounters*. Roy moves toward the aliens and away from his family. Ray moves away from the aliens and towards his children. One narrative is about escape through contact; the other is about responsibility under contact. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2006/great-directors/spielberg/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sensesofcinema.com">[Senses of Cinema]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sensesofcinema.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Senses of CinemaSpielberg, Steven7 Feb 2006 — When Close Encounters becomes a special-effects extravaganza in its final act, Spielberg is...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

## Why Ray Ferrier Matters in Spielberg’s Alien Stories

Ray Ferrier remains distinctive among Spielberg protagonists because he embodies the director’s most pessimistic vision of extraterrestrial encounter. The aliens teach him nothing. They communicate nothing. They inspire no wonder that can be safely embraced. Spielberg himself described the film as an alien story without love or communication, the opposite pole from much of his earlier work. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

What emerges instead is a portrait of fatherhood under extreme pressure. Ray is not remembered because he solves the invasion or uncovers its secrets. He matters because he represents the ordinary person confronting an extraordinary threat with no special knowledge and no heroic destiny.

His achievement is smaller than saving the world but more personal: he learns how to be the father his children need before it is too late. In a film about hostile contact, that becomes the real test. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/105219/1/War%20of%20the%20Worlds%20HH.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: orca.cardiff.ac.uk">[ORCA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">orca.cardiff.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">War of the Worlds HH</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This is not to say that thematizing troubled fatherhood is specific to Spielberg.Read more...</span></span></span>
