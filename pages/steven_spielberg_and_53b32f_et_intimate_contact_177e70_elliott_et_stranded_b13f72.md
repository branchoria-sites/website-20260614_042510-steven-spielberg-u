## Divorce, Loneliness, and the Missing Home

Elliott is introduced as a child whose family is physically present but emotionally incomplete. His father has left, his mother is overwhelmed, and he occupies the awkward position of a middle child struggling to be heard. Spielberg has repeatedly connected the film to his own experience of parental separation, explaining that the story grew from his desire to explore the emotional effects of divorce through science fiction.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nme.com/news/film/steven-spielberg-explains-how-parents-divorce-inspired-e-t-3211364" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nme.com">[NME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nme.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">steven spielberg explains how parents divorce inspired e t 3211364</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Steven Spielberg explains how his parents&#x27; divorce...24 Apr 2022 — Steven Spielberg has explained how his parents&#x27; divorce inspired h...</span></span></span>

E.T. arrives with a parallel loss. He is not an explorer, conqueror or teacher. He is stranded. The film’s entire plot depends on the fact that he has been separated from his family and cannot return home on his own. Elliott’s loneliness and E.T.’s isolation are therefore reflections of one another. Both characters spend the story trying to repair a broken connection.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span>

Critics and scholars have long noted that the film treats Elliott and E.T. as emotional doubles. The boy has lost his father; the alien has lost his people. Each becomes a substitute family for the other. One contemporary analysis described the film as an odyssey of loss centred on a child's instinct to protect another vulnerable being.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://search.proquest.com/openview/a90c91b6289fe8047e373cb8c2226755/1?cbl=1820904&amp;pq-origsite=gscholar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: search.proquest.com">[ProQuest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">search.proquest.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pro Quest&quot;E</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">T.&quot;: AN ODYSSEY OF LOSSby JL Drezner · 1983 · Cited by 5 — Spielberg has established E.T.&#x27;s peril, the need of the universal child to be...</span></span></span>

This mirroring was built into the project from the beginning. Spielberg's original concept emerged from an imaginary companion he created during his own childhood after his parents divorced. That personal history explains why E.T. feels less like a visitor from another world than a friend invented to fill an emotional absence.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span>

## Why the Alien Is Protected Rather Than Feared

Most alien-contact stories begin with uncertainty about whether the visitor is dangerous. *E.T.* reverses that expectation almost immediately. Elliott's first lasting response is not fear but concern.

The reason is structural as much as emotional. Spielberg presents E.T. as a child-sized figure who is frightened, lost and dependent on others. Instead of asking viewers to identify with the adults investigating the phenomenon, the film asks them to identify with the child who discovers it. The question becomes: what responsibility do you have toward someone more vulnerable than yourself?<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://search.proquest.com/openview/a90c91b6289fe8047e373cb8c2226755/1?cbl=1820904&amp;pq-origsite=gscholar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: search.proquest.com">[ProQuest]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">search.proquest.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pro Quest&quot;E</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">T.&quot;: AN ODYSSEY OF LOSSby JL Drezner · 1983 · Cited by 5 — Spielberg has established E.T.&#x27;s peril, the need of the universal child to be...</span></span></span>

Elliott recognises E.T.'s predicament because it resembles his own. Neither character possesses authority. Neither understands the systems surrounding them. Both rely on [secrecy]({{ 'secrecy/' | relative_url }}), trust and a small circle of allies. The friendship therefore develops less as an adventure than as a protective pact.

Screenwriter Melissa Mathison and Spielberg shaped E.T. around qualities associated with kindness and empathy rather than superiority. The alien's abilities are impressive, but his defining characteristic is emotional openness. He heals, communicates and connects. His powers are secondary to his capacity for friendship.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.newyorker.com/culture/culture-desk/the-screenwriter-of-e-t-and-the-bfg-says-goodbye" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newyorker.com">[The New Yorker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newyorker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The New Yorker The Screenwriter of &quot;E.T.&quot; and &quot;The BFG&quot; Says Goodbye</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Extra-Terrestrial.&quot; Mathison&#x27;s approach was highly involved, influencing not just the script but the filming method as well, includin...</span></span></span>

This choice fundamentally changes the meaning of the UFO encounter. The alien is not a mystery to solve. He is a friend to shelter.

## How Shared Vulnerability Changes the UFO Story

The most unusual feature of Elliott and E.T.'s relationship is that neither character is complete without the other.

As their bond deepens, the film creates a literal emotional connection between them. They begin to share sensations, feelings and impulses. Elliott experiences E.T.'s emotions, while E.T. becomes increasingly tied to Elliott's wellbeing. Their famous psychic link is more than a science-fiction device; it externalises the empathy that defines their friendship.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://exhibits.usu.edu/exhibits/show/ettheextraterrestrial/elliottsrelationshipwithet" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: exhibits.usu.edu">[USU Digital Exhibits]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">exhibits.usu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">and Elliott to the point where they feel each other&#x27;s feelings, and as E.T. learns to communicate, we learn that E.T....Read more...</span></span></span>

The connection also changes Elliott himself. Before meeting E.T., he is cautious, withdrawn and burdened by feelings he cannot easily express. Through the relationship, he becomes more confident, more emotionally direct and more willing to act. Critics have argued that E.T. effectively unlocks parts of Elliott's personality that had been suppressed by loneliness and loss.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial</span></span></span>

At the same time, E.T. survives only because Elliott chooses to care for him. Spielberg later described the film as a “double rescue” in which both characters save one another. Elliott gives E.T. a temporary home, while E.T. gives Elliott a sense of connection and purpose.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aiqLxSuXBzI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[YouTube]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Steven Spielberg on E.T.: THE EXTRA-TERRESTRIALDecember 21, 2011 — Steven Spielberg talks about the characters of Elliott and E.T...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 2011</span></span></span>

This reciprocal structure distinguishes *E.T.* from many earlier UFO narratives. In films about alien visitation, humans often seek knowledge, power or proof. Here, the central exchange is emotional. What passes between Elliott and E.T. is not information but companionship.
