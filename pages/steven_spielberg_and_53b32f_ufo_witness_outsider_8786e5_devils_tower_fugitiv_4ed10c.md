## Introduction

In *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind*, the final movement at [Devils Tower]({{ 'devils-tower/' | relative_url }}) transforms the UFO witness from a social outsider into a political outsider. Roy Neary and Jillian Guiler are no longer merely people whose experiences are doubted. By the time they reach Wyoming, they have become individuals excluded from a state-managed event that concerns them more directly than anyone else. Spielberg stages the climax as a conflict between personal knowledge and official control: the government knows the UFOs are coming, but it also decides who may witness that arrival and who must be kept away. Roy and Jillian therefore arrive at first contact not as invited participants but as fugitives. Their flight toward Devils Tower turns the witness narrative into a struggle over access, legitimacy and authority.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

## The False Emergency and Public Exclusion

The decisive act of exclusion occurs when authorities identify Devils Tower as the UFO rendezvous site and create a massive security operation around it. In the film, the area is evacuated through a fabricated public emergency: news reports claim that a train accident has released a dangerous toxic nerve gas. Roads are blocked, residents are removed and access is restricted while military and scientific personnel prepare the landing zone.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Recap/CloseEncountersOfTheThirdKind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tvtropes.org">[TV Tropes]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tvtropes.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind RecapThe Army evacuates the entire area around Devils Tower by planting false reports in the media tha...</span></span></span>

This deception matters because Spielberg could have used a straightforward military quarantine. Instead, he chooses a false narrative directed at the public. The government does not simply secure the site; it manufactures consent for exclusion. People are not told that extraterrestrial contact is imminent. They are told a story designed to make them leave voluntarily. Roy and Jillian become unusual because they possess enough knowledge to recognise the lie. When they see Devils Tower on television, they realise that the mountain from their visions is real and that the official explanation cannot account for their experiences.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.filmsite.org/clos3.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: filmsite.org">[filmsite]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">filmsite.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)Ignoring the restrictions and news reports about toxic nerve gas - an... ## Why Refusal Turns Witnesses into Trespassers

Roy and Jillian do not become fugitives because they wish to challenge the state. They become fugitives because compliance would require them to deny what they know.

Throughout the film, both characters have been driven by a recurring mental image of Devils Tower. The mountain functions as evidence that cannot be shared through conventional channels. Others see obsession or irrationality. Roy and Jillian experience certainty. When the evacuation begins, the authorities effectively demand that they abandon the destination that has dominated their lives since their encounters. Their refusal converts them from [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) into trespassers.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

Spielberg carefully emphasises this transition. Roy and Jillian travel alongside crowds fleeing the supposed disaster, moving against the direction of public behaviour. They are stopped, questioned and eventually intercepted by military personnel. Other vision-driven travellers are removed from the area, while Roy and Jillian escape and continue toward the restricted zone. Their actions are technically unlawful within the framework created by the authorities, yet the audience understands that their pursuit is justified because the official framework itself is based on deception.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://crazyfilmguy.blogspot.com/2017/07/close-encounters-of-third-kind-1977.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: crazyfilmguy.blogspot.com">[Crazy Film Guy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">crazyfilmguy.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">close encounters of third kind 1977</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Crazy Film GuyClose Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)30 Jul 2017 — Roy sees the news footage of Devil&#x27;s Tower and realizes that&#x27;s the i...</span></span></span>

The result is a familiar Spielberg pattern: ordinary people acquire knowledge that institutions cannot comfortably accommodate. Roy is not an expert, scientist or government official. His authority comes entirely from experience. That authority has no recognised place inside the official system, so he must operate outside it.

### The Boundary as a Test of Belief

Devils Tower is not merely a location. It is a boundary.

The mountain separates two worlds: the evacuated public sphere below and the secret contact zone above. To cross that boundary, Roy and Jillian must ignore warnings, bypass checkpoints and climb into territory where they are not supposed to be. The ascent turns belief into action. Earlier in the film, Roy could obsess privately. At Devils Tower, conviction demands risk.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.scottholleran.com/movies/movie-analysis-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-1977/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scottholleran.com">[Scott Holleran]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scottholleran.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">movie analysis close encounters of the third kind 1977</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Movie Analysis: Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)4 Sept 2017 — That two freethinkers escape government control in that gas-guzzli...</span></span></span>

This is why the climax feels different from a conventional conspiracy thriller. Roy is not trying to expose a hidden programme. He is trying to reach an event that he already knows is real. The dramatic question is not whether the UFOs exist. It is whether the witness will be allowed to stand in the place where truth becomes visible.

## Contact as Vindication at the Boundary

When Roy and Jillian finally reach the observation area, Spielberg resolves the outsider narrative through direct experience rather than public proof.

The UFOs arrive exactly where the witnesses believed they would. The visions were accurate. The compulsion was meaningful. The mountain was a genuine summons rather than a delusion. Everything that isolated Roy from his family and community is retrospectively validated by the appearance of the craft above Devils Tower.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

Yet Spielberg does not portray this vindication as a triumph over the authorities. The climax is more complicated. Scientists and government personnel are also present, and they successfully establish communication through sound and light. The film does not argue that institutions are wholly wrong. Instead, it shows that institutional knowledge and witness knowledge arrive at the same destination through different paths. The authorities possess resources, technology and organisational power. Roy and Jillian possess experiential certainty. Both prove necessary to the encounter.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

The crucial difference is that the witnesses reach the truth despite exclusion. Their legitimacy comes from persistence rather than permission.
