## Introduction

For Spielberg, the importance of UFO [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) has never depended on proving every claim true. What mattered was the recurring pattern: ordinary people from different backgrounds describing events that profoundly affected them. Understanding why those stories resonated helps explain how his belief evolved over time.

## The Witness Interviews Behind *Close Encounters*

Before *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* became a science-fiction landmark, Spielberg was interested in making a project centred on people who believed they had encountered UFOs. Early versions of the concept focused less on alien visitors themselves and more on the individuals who reported seeing them. The film's eventual structure retained that focus by making its central character an ordinary worker whose life is transformed by an unexplained sighting. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

Spielberg immersed himself in UFO literature and sought guidance from prominent investigators. Astronomer and UFO researcher J. Allen Hynek became a consultant on the film after Columbia acquired rights connected to his work. Hynek's famous “close encounter” classification system supplied the film's title and helped ground its fictional story in real-world witness reports. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://catalog.afi.com/Film/67160-CLOSE-ENCOUNTERS-OF-THE-THIRD-KIND" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: catalog.afi.com">[AFI Catalog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">catalog.afi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AFI CatalogClose Encounters of the Third Kind (1977) - AFI CatalogA settlement resulted in Columbia purchasing the rights to Hynek&#x27;s book...</span></span></span>

Many reports described people who struggled to explain what they had seen, became preoccupied with the experience, or felt isolated because others doubted them. ## Why Ordinary Observers Felt Credible

A defining feature of Spielberg's UFO storytelling is that it rarely begins with scientists, generals or intelligence officials. Instead, it begins with teachers, parents, workers and children. The protagonist [Roy Neary]({{ 'roy-neary/' | relative_url }}) was deliberately conceived as an everyday person rather than a heroic expert. Spielberg himself described wanting an “everyday regular fella” at the centre of the story. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

This choice reflected a broader pattern in the witness material that interested him. UFO reports often came from people with no obvious reason to seek publicity and no professional stake in extraordinary claims. Their stories carried emotional weight precisely because they appeared mundane before the reported encounter.

For Spielberg, [credibility]({{ 'credibility/' | relative_url }}) seems to have operated on two levels:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Emotional credibility:** witnesses often sounded sincere, frightened, confused or transformed rather than theatrical.
* **Cultural credibility:** similar stories emerged across different regions and generations.
* **Narrative credibility:** many accounts focused on uncertainty rather than certainty, with witnesses admitting they did not know what they had seen.

</div>

These qualities did not constitute scientific proof. Yet they helped create the impression that a significant human phenomenon existed even if its cause remained unclear. That distinction became a recurring feature of Spielberg's approach to UFOs. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/close-encounter-with-steven-spielberg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bfi.org.uk">[BFI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bfi.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">close encounter with steven spielberg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BFIA close encounter with Steven Spielberg &#124; Sight and Sound10 Dec 2021 — We joined Spielberg on the set of his fourth feature, Close Enc...</span></span></span>

## How Decades of Testimony Shaped His Belief

The most revealing aspect of Spielberg's recent comments is that he framed his belief as cumulative. He did not cite a breakthrough document, a recovered artefact or a single famous case. Instead, he described a lifetime spent absorbing accounts from witnesses and researchers. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fW73jP8RolM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CBS News...</span></span></span>

This matters because it shows a different route to belief than the one usually discussed in UFO debates. Scientists typically ask whether a specific piece of evidence can withstand rigorous testing. Spielberg's reasoning appears closer to a long-term pattern-recognition process. Over decades, he encountered thousands of reports, interviews, investigations and public testimonies. Eventually, he concluded that the sheer volume of sincere accounts constituted persuasive circumstantial evidence. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fW73jP8RolM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CBS News...</span></span></span>

His 2026 remarks suggest that congressional hearings and contemporary testimony contributed to that accumulation, but they were additions to a much older foundation rather than the starting point. The witness stories that fascinated him in the 1970s remained part of the same evidential chain. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fW73jP8RolM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CBS News...</span></span></span>

## Witnesses as a Cultural Lens Rather Than Proof

An important nuance in Spielberg's position is that he has consistently separated respect for witnesses from claims of definitive proof. He has acknowledged that he has never personally seen a UFO. His conviction therefore rests largely on the experiences reported by others. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fW73jP8RolM" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CBS News...</span></span></span>

That perspective helps explain why witnesses remained central to his UFO imagination for nearly fifty years. Whether viewed as evidence of extraterrestrial visitation, misunderstood natural phenomena, psychological experiences or something else entirely, witness accounts revealed a recurring human encounter with mystery. Spielberg's films repeatedly return to that moment when an ordinary person confronts something they cannot explain.
