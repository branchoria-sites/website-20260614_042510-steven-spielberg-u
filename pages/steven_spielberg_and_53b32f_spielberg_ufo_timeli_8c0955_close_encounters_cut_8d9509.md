## Introduction

Few decisions in Steven Spielberg’s UFO career reveal his artistic priorities more clearly than the changing versions of *[Close Encounters]({{ 'close-encounters/' | relative_url }}) of the Third Kind*. The film exists in three major forms: the 1977 theatrical release, the 1980 Special Edition, and the 1998 Collector’s Edition/Director’s Cut. What changed was not merely running time. The revisions exposed a fundamental dilemma: should a filmmaker show audiences the ultimate UFO revelation, or preserve the mystery that makes it powerful in the first place?

For Spielberg, the answer changed over time. The 1980 version attempted to reveal more, including a glimpse inside the alien mothership. By the late 1990s, he had concluded that this disclosure weakened the film’s sense of wonder. The evolution of the film’s cuts became a practical experiment in how much of the unknown should remain unseen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

## Should the mothership stay unknowable?

The original 1977 film ends with [Roy Neary]({{ 'roy-neary/' | relative_url }}) boarding the enormous alien craft after humanity’s first peaceful contact. The audience [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) the spectacle from outside. The mothership remains a mystery. Viewers know Roy is entering another world, but they are not invited inside with him.

That ending matched the film’s broader approach to UFOs. Throughout *Close Encounters*, Spielberg repeatedly suggests more than he explains. Strange lights appear before their source is visible. Communication arrives through music and pattern rather than exposition. The climax offers contact without a detailed explanation of alien civilisation. The unknown remains intact.

Yet Spielberg was dissatisfied with aspects of the rushed 1977 release. Columbia Pictures had pressed for a Christmas launch, and the director later said he wanted more time to finish the film. After its success, the studio agreed to fund additional work, but with a condition: a new version needed a major selling point. The result was the 1980 Special Edition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_%282022_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close (2022 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close (2022 film)It follows two teenage boys whose close friendship is thrown into disarray when their schoolmates notice their intima...</span></span></span>

## What the Special Edition added

The 1980 Special Edition incorporated new scenes, revised character moments and additional visual material. Its most famous addition came at the very end.

After Roy enters the mothership, the audience briefly sees the interior of the alien craft. Bright corridors, glowing structures and otherworldly spaces replace the blank mystery left by the theatrical version. The sequence gave viewers exactly what science-fiction audiences are often denied: a look behind the curtain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span>

From a commercial perspective, the addition made sense. Columbia could market the reissue around a promise that audiences would finally see what had previously been hidden. Contemporary advertising emphasised this new access, turning the interior of the mothership into the Special Edition’s central attraction.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://thehdroom.com/reviews/close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-30th-anniversary-ultimate-edition-blu-ray-review-30611/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thehdroom.com">[TheHDRoom]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thehdroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind (30th Anniversary...24 Apr 2008 — The 1980 Special Edition trailer runs about two minutes, f...</span></span></span>

The change also reflected a common temptation in visual storytelling. Once a filmmaker has imagined an extraordinary object, there is a natural desire to reveal it. The audience has invested two hours waiting for answers. Showing more can feel like delivering on that promise.

For a time, Spielberg accepted that logic.

## Why later cuts restored mystery

By the 1990s, Spielberg had reconsidered.

When preparing what became the 1998 Collector’s Edition, he removed the interior mothership footage. Although the new cut retained selected material from the Special Edition, the sequence showing the inside of the alien craft disappeared. Spielberg stated that the interior should have remained a mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_%282022_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close (2022 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close (2022 film)It follows two teenage boys whose close friendship is thrown into disarray when their schoolmates notice their intima...</span></span></span>

His explanation has become one of the most revealing comments he has made about cinematic wonder. Reflecting on the compromise that led to the 1980 version, Spielberg said, “I never should have shown the inside of the mothership.” He later argued that the unseen interior was more powerful than any visualisation he could create.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_%282022_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close (2022 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close (2022 film)It follows two teenage boys whose close friendship is thrown into disarray when their schoolmates notice their intima...</span></span></span>

The 1998 cut therefore became a correction rather than simply another revision. It preserved many improvements Spielberg liked from the Special Edition while restoring the uncertainty of the original ending. Roy still enters the craft, but audiences once again stop at the threshold. The unknown survives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_%282022_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close (2022 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close (2022 film)It follows two teenage boys whose close friendship is thrown into disarray when their schoolmates notice their intima...</span></span></span>

This decision is especially significant within Spielberg’s UFO work. *Close Encounters* is fundamentally about contact with something beyond ordinary human understanding. Revealing too much risks transforming transcendence into production design.

## How showing too much changes awe

The debate over the three versions highlights a larger principle that extends beyond one film.

Awe often depends on incompleteness. The imagination can construct possibilities larger than any visual effect. Once the audience sees a concrete answer, the mystery narrows. What was limitless becomes specific.

The mothership interior demonstrates this effect. In the theatrical version, viewers project their own ideas onto the unseen space. It could be magnificent, terrifying, spiritual or incomprehensible. The Special Edition replaces those possibilities with a particular design. Whether impressive or not, it becomes finite.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_%282022_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close (2022 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close (2022 film)It follows two teenage boys whose close friendship is thrown into disarray when their schoolmates notice their intima...</span></span></span>

Many critics and later commentators have argued that the restored ambiguity strengthens the ending because the film’s emotional climax is Roy’s decision to leave Earth, not a guided tour of alien architecture. The mystery becomes part of the payoff rather than an obstacle to it.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.movie-censorship.com/report.php?ID=101920" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: movie-censorship.com">[Movie Censorship]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">movie-censorship.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">There is more upbeat music in the Special Edition, the Director&#x27;s Cut features dark, serious music.Read more...</span></span></span>

This is why the different cuts matter within Spielberg’s UFO [timeline]({{ 'timeline/' | relative_url }}). They reveal a filmmaker testing two competing instincts:

* **The urge to reveal**: giving audiences access to the extraordinary.
* **The urge to withhold**: preserving the power of the unknown.

The 1980 Special Edition leaned toward revelation. The 1998 version moved back toward restraint.
